Mazal

Your Mazal Is More Than a Zodiac Sign

In Jewish sources, mazal does not mean fate. The word is connected to the idea of a flow or channel of influence from Above. Each Hebrew month carries a traditional mazal, and Jewish tradition speaks of a person's mazal shining with particular strength on their Hebrew birthday.

But a Jew is not bound by the stars. The Talmud records the teaching אין מזל לישראל — "there is no mazal for Israel" — in a discussion of whether planetary influence determines a Jew's fate. Rabbi Yochanan holds that Israel is not subject to it, and the Talmud brings the example of Avraham, who was told to look beyond his astrological reading. Through prayer, teshuvah, tzedakah, Torah and free choice, a person is never trapped by any constellation.

Established source Talmud, Shabbat 156a ↗
Commentators differ on how far this teaching extends — some understand it as removing astrological influence entirely, others as saying that merit and prayer can override it. We present the teaching and its source rather than settling the debate.

So the month's mazal below is offered as symbolism to reflect on, never as a prediction. We will never tell you that because you were born under a certain sign you definitely are a certain way.

Your Tehillim

Your Personal Tehillim Chapter

According to a widely-followed Chassidic custom, a person recites the chapter of Tehillim matching the year of life they have just entered. A person turning 23 enters their 24th year and begins saying Tehillim 24.

This chapter comes from your age, not from your name's gematria. Any connection between a name's number and a psalm would be a separate creative reflection, not this custom.
